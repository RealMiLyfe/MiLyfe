# MiLyfe Uniform Configuration Manual

**Status:** Design authority
**Product name:** MiLyfe
**Brand law:** Every MiLyfe product, OS, cell, service, screen, workflow, agent, device experience, and public-facing system is part of the MiLyfe world.
**Mode:** Design and governance only; no code

## 1. Purpose

This manual defines how every part of MiLyfe is named, branded, configured, localized, operated, evolved, white-labeled, and connected.

The purpose is uniformity without centralizing ownership.

MiLyfe may appear through many OSes, communities, cells, ventures, campaigns, devices, and domains, but the user should always be able to understand:

- This is part of MiLyfe.
- Which OS is responsible.
- Who owns or stewards the current object.
- Which permissions are being used.
- What happened.
- How to get help.
- How to export, leave, pause, or appeal.

## 2. Naming law

### 2.1 Canonical master name

The master brand is always written:

> **MiLyfe**

The capitalization and spelling are not optional in official product surfaces.

Do not use alternate product-wide labels that make the platform appear unrelated to MiLyfe.

### 2.2 Sub-brand law

Domain OSes use the MiLyfe naming family:

- MiForge
- MiMoney
- MiScale
- MiKnowledge
- MiMedia
- MiHealth
- MiJustice
- MiResolve
- MiLegal
- MiElection
- MiPublic Office
- MiDevice
- MiData
- MiCare

A sub-brand may have a descriptive local or public name, but the MiLyfe relationship remains visible.

Recommended presentation:

> **MiForge**  
> A MiLyfe system for creating ventures, agents, services, and businesses.

### 2.3 External identity law

External projects and integrations keep their real names. MiLyfe must not falsely rename or claim ownership of third-party software.

Examples:

- PeerTube — connected to MiMedia
- Apache Fineract — optional MiBank/MiCredit adapter
- Mojaloop — optional payment interoperability adapter
- CiviCRM — optional Campaign OS adapter

The interface may show:

> Connected through MiLyfe

or:

> MiLyfe integration

but it must not imply that the external project is owned by MiLyfe.

## 3. Living network identity

MiLyfe is designed as a voluntary, people-owned network and commons of people, communities, cooperatives, builders, agents, devices, services, and cells.

The architecture does not require the platform to be designed as a state or as a single centrally owned company before it can exist as a technical and social system.

However, this does not create automatic legal immunity or exemption from external law. When MiLyfe contracts, hires, sells, handles money, serves people, publishes campaigns, operates devices, or interacts with public institutions, the relevant jurisdictional and professional requirements must be represented honestly through MiLegal and the applicable OS.

MiLyfe can be self-governing in its internal rules while remaining accurate about its external relationships.

## 4. Configuration hierarchy

Configuration is recursive. Every setting belongs to a level.

### Level 0 — MiLyfe invariants

These apply everywhere and cannot be overridden by a local cell, white-label package, campaign, agent, or operator.

- Canonical MiLyfe identity
- Human and community final authority over reserved actions
- One authoritative `$MLY` monetary authority
- No hidden central custody of everyone’s `$MLY`
- Receipts for consequential actions
- Data ownership and export
- Offline and degraded-mode disclosure
- Accessibility baseline
- Child and vulnerable-person safeguards
- Encryption and security baseline
- MiResolve/MiLegal/MiJustice separation
- Campaign/election/public-office separation
- Device owner permission
- No negative balances
- Pause and revocation
- Removal and fork behavior

## 8. Configuration and AI agents

An AI agent may propose or prepare configuration changes but may not silently activate high-impact configuration.

Required agent workflow:

1. Read the MiLyfe configuration manual.
2. Identify the configuration level.
3. Identify the invariants affected.
4. Produce a human-readable diff.
5. Identify affected people, OSes, cells, data, money, permissions, and devices.
6. Run validation and compatibility checks.
7. Request human or community approval.
8. Stage the change.
9. Monitor results.
10. Issue a receipt.
11. Roll back or correct when required.

Agents may not:

- Rename MiLyfe without approval
- Remove MiLyfe safety or accessibility controls
- Expand money authority
- Remove receipts
- Grant themselves capabilities
- Change campaign, election, public-office, justice, or governance authority
- Hide the owner or steward of a resource
- Delete a configuration history

## 12. Living-system evolution

MiLyfe is treated as a living system in the operational sense:

- It learns from verified experience.
- It grows through people, communities, ventures, devices, and services.
- It evolves through reviewed configuration and code changes.
- It preserves its history.
- It supports succession.
- It can split or fork.
- It can remove a failing OS without destroying the entire network.
- It can continue after any founder, operator, campaign, or vendor leaves.

The living-system metaphor does not remove accountability. Every change still requires ownership, scope, review, receipts, and a recovery path.

## 13. What remains external

MiLyfe may operate as a voluntary people-owned network and internal commons. It should not falsely represent itself as automatically sovereign, immune, incorporated, licensed, or exempt from external rules.

External legal, tax, financial, professional, employment, health, election, device, and public-office relationships are configured through jurisdiction profiles and reviewed through MiLegal and the relevant domain OS.

This preserves both:

- Internal people-owned autonomy
- Honest external representation
